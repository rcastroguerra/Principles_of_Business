# **Question:** 5

## True or False Question

**It is illegal for a potential employer to ask an applicant about his or her social media accounts, like Facebook.**

- O True
- False

---

## Correct Answer

**False**

---

## Explanation

In most jurisdictions (including the United States at the federal level), there is **no blanket federal law** that makes it illegal for an employer to simply *ask* an applicant about their social media accounts, such as Facebook.

However, the legal landscape is nuanced:

- **Asking for login credentials (passwords)** is illegal in many states (over half of U.S. states have laws prohibiting employers from demanding usernames or passwords to personal social media accounts).

- **Asking to view public social media content** is generally legal because the information is already publicly available.

- **Asking about social media accounts in a discriminatory way** (e.g., only asking candidates of a certain race, religion, or age) could violate anti-discrimination laws (e.g., Title VII of the Civil Rights Act).

- **Using social media information to make hiring decisions** could be legal or illegal depending on *what* is discovered (e.g., discovering someone's religion, disability, pregnancy, or age and using that to not hire them would be illegal).

**Key distinction:**  
The question states: *"ask an applicant about his or her social media accounts"* (not asking for passwords). Merely asking *if* someone has an account or asking to *view* public information is generally **not** illegal under federal law, though some state laws may restrict certain types of social media inquiries.

Therefore, the statement is **False** because it is not universally illegal to ask about social media accounts. The legality depends on *how* the question is asked (e.g., demanding passwords vs. viewing public content) and state-specific laws.

# **Question:** 22

## True or False Question

**Assessing an employee's performance is one of the most difficult tasks for managers.**

- O True
- O False

---

## Correct Answer

**True**

---

## Explanation

Performance appraisal is widely recognized by HR professionals and management researchers as one of the most challenging tasks managers face. Reasons include:

- **Subjectivity and bias** → Managers may struggle with rating errors such as leniency, strictness, central tendency, or recency bias (focusing on recent events rather than the entire evaluation period).

- **Halo/horn effect** → A single positive trait (halo) or negative trait (horn) can unfairly influence the overall rating of an employee.

- **Emotional difficulty** → Providing constructive criticism or negative feedback can be uncomfortable for managers, especially when it affects promotions, pay, or job security.

- **Lack of objective metrics** → For many jobs (especially knowledge-based or service roles), performance is not easily measured with hard numbers, making fair assessment difficult.

- **Time-consuming** → Effective performance appraisal requires ongoing observation, documentation, and thoughtful evaluation, which many managers struggle to fit into their schedules.

- **Legal risk** → Poorly conducted appraisals can lead to claims of discrimination or unfair treatment, adding pressure to get it right.

Therefore, the statement is **True** — assessing employee performance is indeed one of the most difficult tasks for managers.
