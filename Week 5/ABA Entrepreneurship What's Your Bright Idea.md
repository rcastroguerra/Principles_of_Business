### Opciones

- A product that the owner has not tested on a small scale, requires virtually no time commitment, and is financially feasible.

- A product that is very challenging to create, pertains to a saturated market, and is financially feasible.

- **A product that is of great interest to the owner, has customer demand, and is financially feasible.**


## Correct Answer

**Option 3**  
*A product that is of great interest to the owner, has customer demand, and is financially feasible*

### Why Option 3 is Correct

**Option 3:** *A product that is of great interest to the owner, has customer demand, and is financially feasible*

This option represents the three fundamental pillars of a successful small business:

- **Great interest to the owner:** If you are not passionate about the product, you will quit when problems arise. Passion keeps you motivated.
- **Customer demand:** It is not enough that you like it. There must be people willing to pay for it.
- **Financially feasible:** The business must generate enough revenue to cover costs and be sustainable over time.

**Real-world example:** A gardener who loves plants (interest), lives in an area where people pay for gardening services (demand), and can charge more than the cost of pots and their time (feasibility).

---

### Why Option 1 is Incorrect

**Option 1:** *A product that the owner has not tested on a small scale, requires virtually no time commitment, and is financially feasible*

- **Not tested on a small scale:** Launching without testing is extremely risky. Small-scale testing reveals problems before you invest a lot of money.
- **Virtually no time commitment:** This is unrealistic. Any serious business requires time and effort, especially at the beginning.

This option describes a fantasy, not a real business. It ignores the hard work and validation necessary for success.

---

### Why Option 2 is Incorrect

**Option 2:** *A product that is very challenging to create, pertains to a saturated market, and is financially feasible*

- **Very challenging to create:** High difficulty increases time, cost, and the risk of failing before you even launch.
- **Saturated market:** Lots of existing competition. Without a clear advantage, it is very difficult to gain customers.

Combining "very challenging to create" with "saturated market" is a double disadvantage. High barriers to entry + intense competition = very difficult to succeed.
